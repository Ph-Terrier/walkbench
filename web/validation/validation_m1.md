## 1. Scope

The page computes model M1 and its outcomes with the JavaScript files of `js/`. The validation compares them with walkbench 0.7.0 at three levels:

- the dynamics: fixed point, linearization, runs with an input series taken from R, falls;
- the computations on values taken from R: divergence curve, λS and λL, DFA, ARFIMA inputs, fixed series and the generator of random numbers;
- the whole chain, simulation and estimation in JavaScript, on the input series of a run of walkbench.

Section 6 lists the limits of the validation.

## 2. Test vectors

`tools/01_make_vectors_m1.R` computed, with walkbench:

- the fixed point of `model_m1()` and the linearization of `linearize()`;
- five ARFIMA inputs of `input_arfima()`, with the normal draws that produced them, for d from −0.30 to 0.45;
- a synthetic series of 300 values passed through `input_fixed_series()`;
- a run of 620 steps (seed 1, d = 0.3, σ = 2.34 × 10⁻³ rad, the reference amplitude of M1 in the analysis plan), with its step events, samples and outcomes of `estimate_all()`;
- the same run and the fixed point computed by the frozen functions of M1 at the lsodar tolerances rtol 1e-13 and atol 1e-15, without a change of the package (the "tight reference");
- uniform and normal draws of R after `set.seed()` for five seeds;
- a run of 130 steps with its whole trajectory, and its divergence exponents under the settings of the plan and under the windows 0 to 0.2 and 4 to 6 steps;
- a run that falls (seed 1001, d = 0, σ = 0.006 rad), at step 40;
- the DFA alpha of five series, one of them too short.

The series are synthetic or generated by walkbench; the vectors contain no third-party data.

## 3. Tolerances

The author chose, on 2026-11-19, the two-level tolerances put by the orchestrating model. After the first run of the tests, the author chose the tight reference for the fixed point and for the cumulative times of the steps (section 4). After the validation of M2, the author chose the same reference for the sampled states. Every difference is an absolute difference.

| Level | Quantity | Reference | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| dynamics | fixed point (θ, θ̇) and period | tight reference | 1e-9 |
| dynamics | moduli of the eigenvalues of the linearized step map | walkbench | 1e-4 |
| dynamics | run with a fixed input: step durations, their cumulative sums, sampled states | tight reference | 1e-7 |
| dynamics | run with a fixed input: post-strike states, falls | walkbench | 1e-7, same falls |
| same values as R | estimators, ARFIMA filter, fixed series, generator | walkbench and R | 1e-9 |
| end to end | λS and λL | walkbench | 1e-4 |
| end to end | α | walkbench | 1e-6 |

The orchestrating model placed the ARFIMA filter, the fixed series and the generator under the level "same values as R", since their inputs come from R.

## 4. Results

The full table is in `validation/results_m1.md`. The main results:

- Fixed point. Against the tight reference, the fixed point and the period agree to 1.2 × 10⁻¹² (T1). Against walkbench at its default tolerances, they differ by 1.93 × 10⁻⁹ (T1b, information).
- Runs. Over 620 steps and against the tight reference, the step durations agree to 1.5 × 10⁻¹¹ and their cumulative sums to 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ (T5, T5f). The sampled states of six steps agree to 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ (T5e). Against walkbench, the post-strike states agree to 9.5 × 10⁻¹¹ (T5b), and the sampled states to 7.8 × 10⁻⁸ (T5h, information). The runs have the same 80,207 samples, the same legs and no fall. The run with a fall stops at step 40 in both codes (T6).
- Estimators on the values of R. The divergence curves agree to 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ and λS and λL to 9.3 × 10⁻¹⁶ (T7, T8). All 14,231 nearest neighbors are the same, and so are the numbers of pairs, the horizons and the Theiler windows. The DFA alpha agrees to 2.8 × 10⁻¹⁶ (T9).
- Reading of a file (M1 and M2, since 2026-11-21). `tests/run_tests_files.js` reads 17 test files of `tools/04_make_vectors_files.R` (`validation/results_files.md`). The page accepts 6 files and refuses 11, among them the file with decimal commas, each time with the expected lines (F1, F6). Each accepted file is read by `scan()` of R with the same number of values (F3). After `input_fixed_series()`, the series of the page and of walkbench agree to 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁸ (F4). Of nine series, the page classes each one as expected: too few values, all values equal, or valid (F7).
- Inputs and generator. The ARFIMA inputs agree to 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶ and the fixed series to 4.3 × 10⁻¹⁹ (T3, T4). The uniform draws of the page equal those of `runif()`, and its normal draws agree with those of `rnorm()` to 8.9 × 10⁻¹⁶ (T13, T13b). From the seed alone, the page draws the ARFIMA inputs of `input_arfima()` to 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶ (T13c).
- End to end. λS is 0.601477 in JavaScript and 0.601469 in walkbench; λL is 0.030675 and 0.030680. The largest difference is 2.8 × 10⁻⁵ (T10). The alpha values agree to 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ (T10b). The numbers of complete pairs are 76,804 and 76,803 (T10d).
- Linearization. The moduli of the eigenvalues agree to 8.1 × 10⁻⁹ (T2).

## 5. Cause of the differences with walkbench at its default tolerances

The JavaScript integration has converged. With substeps of 1e-3, 5e-4 and 2.5e-4 time units, the period changes by less than 1e-13.

At its default tolerances (rtol 1e-10, atol 1e-12), walkbench gives a period shorter by 1.94 × 10⁻⁹ than the tight reference (T12). Its step durations are shorter by 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ on average over the first 30 steps, and this bias accumulates to 2.1 × 10⁻⁷ at the end of the run of 620 steps (T12b). These differences come from the integration error of walkbench at its default tolerances.

## 6. Limits of the validation

- The indicative values of the animation (`js/wb_stream.js`) come from a different method and are not compared with walkbench.
- The interface was tried on 2026-11-19 in the browser pane of the Claude desktop app (Chromium), with a local web server. The trial covered the animation, the reference run, the redraws after falls, a local file with a synthetic series, the change of language and the export of R code.
- The page was also opened from the file system in Microsoft Edge without display (headless). Its scripts ran under the Content-Security-Policy, without errors. Other browsers were not tried.
- On 2026-11-20, after a first trial of the author in Chrome, the orchestrating model changed the layout of the page and the amplitude of the preset "Beyond σmax" (`README.md`). It left the JavaScript files of the models, of the inputs and of the estimators unchanged, and it did not rerun the checks of this report. Later the same day, it changed the grid count of the resampling for walkbench 0.7.0, which M1 does not use, and reran the checks with the same results.
- On 2026-11-21, after the decision of the author, the same model changed the reading of a file (`js/wb_input.js`, `js/wb_app.js`, `js/wb_i18n.js`). The former reading kept the last number of each line, and a line `0,123` became 123. The checks of this report gave the same values afterwards. In the browser pane, the page refused two files and gave their line numbers, in French and in English: a file with decimal commas, and a file with a header and two columns. A valid file of 600 values was accepted, and the exported R code held the new comment on the format of the file.
- `scan()` of R does not always convert decimal text to the nearest double. In 1 of the 10,760 test values, R and the page differ by one unit in the last binary place (F5, relative difference 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁶).
- Later on 2026-11-21, after four decisions of the author, the same model changed four texts of the page; the checks of this report gave the same values afterwards. In the browser pane, the attempts 1 to 3 of a reference run of M1 fell (σ = 0.00586 rad, d = 0, seed 1). The status line showed the seeds 1 to 4, and the exported R code gave `seed = 4`. A run with the seed 10 did not fall: the status line showed the seed 10, as the R code did. A file of 600 values equal to 0.1 received the message for equal values, in French and in English.
